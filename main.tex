\begin{abstract}
Hundertmark, Pattakos and Schulz introduced a mean--field constant $\beta_s$ in their refinement of the atomic excess--charge bound.  For $1\le s\le 3$ they reduced the variational problem to radial probability measures, while for $s=3$ their explicit numerical theorem used a pointwise lower bound on $\beta_3$.  We solve the remaining one--dimensional radial variational problem at $s=3$ exactly.  After passing to logarithmic radius and an exponentially tilted measure, the minimization becomes a maximization problem for the translation--invariant kernel
\[
 k(t)=\ee^{-|t|}-\ee^{-2|t|}.
\]
The normalized maximizer of the radial logarithmic problem is unique, is supported on a finite interval, and has cosine density.  Writing
\[
 a_*:=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\arctan\sqrt2,
\]
we obtain
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \beta_3=\frac{3\ee^{2a_*}}{1+3\ee^{2a_*}}
 =0.9205346822904167\ldots}
\]
and hence $\beta_3^{-1}=1.086325175181735\ldots$.  We then propagate this exact constant through the symbolic finite--$N$ smearing and weighted kinetic estimates of Hundertmark--Pattakos--Schulz, without introducing a new many--body inequality.  For every $Z\ge4$ this gives the explicit finite--$Z$ bound
\[
\begin{split}
N_c(Z)<{}&\beta_3^{-1}Z+3.812\,Z^{1/3}+0.01294\\
&+0.18185\,Z^{-1/3}+0.01941\,Z^{-2/3},
\end{split}
\]
and in particular
\[
 N_c(Z)<1.086326\,Z+3.90\,Z^{1/3}.
\]
At the level of the leading linear coefficient, this improves the HPS value $1.1185$ to $1.086326$ and removes about $27\%$ of the gap between the HPS coefficient and the conjectural coefficient $1$, while leaving the many--body estimates unchanged.  The explicit extremizer also identifies the radial geometry responsible for the remaining cubic mean--field loss: a compactly supported cosine modulation of the critical $r^{-4}$ profile in logarithmic radius.  Thus the result both sharpens the quantitative excess--charge bound within the HPS cubic framework and isolates which part of the remaining obstruction cannot be removed by scalar radial mean--field optimization alone.
\end{abstract}
