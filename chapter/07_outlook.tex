\section{Outlook: beyond the mean--field constant}\label{sec:outlook}

The exact computation of $\beta_3$ resolves only the one--dimensional radial mean--field subproblem.  It does not by itself prove the ionization conjecture.  Nevertheless, it sharpens the place where additional many--fermion information must enter.

In the weighted $s=3$ multiplier identity, the Coulomb contribution is accompanied by positive terms that are discarded in the purely geometric estimate.  Schematically one has
\[
 R_3+S_3+A_3+I_Nm_3=Zm_2+m_1,
\]
where $R_3$ is the weighted electron--electron repulsion, $S_3$ is a critical Hardy surplus, $A_3$ is the excitation energy of the $(N-1)$--electron core, and $I_N$ is the ionization energy.  The exact value of $\beta_3$ shows that the purely radial mean--field obstruction corresponds to a much smaller gap than the pointwise pair estimate suggests.

The extremizer found here is particularly suggestive.  Its three--dimensional density is a compactly supported cosine modulation of $r^{-4}$.  The power $r^{-4}$ is the density associated with the critical zero mode of the weighted Hardy operator arising at $s=3$.  Thus a one--particle Hardy argument alone cannot be expected to exclude near--extremal Coulomb geometry: the two structures are nearly compatible.  The missing mechanism should instead exploit fermionic occupation constraints.  In logarithmic radius, this leads naturally to a spectral filling problem on a cylinder, where Pauli exclusion forces higher radial or angular modes once the near--zero mode is occupied.

A plausible next step is therefore a stability inequality of the form
\[
 \mathcal G_3[\Psi]+\mathcal H_3[\Psi]
 \ge \delta\,(1-\beta_3)(N-1)m_2-\mathcal R_N,
\]
where $\mathcal G_3$ measures the excess over the exact radial mean--field minimum, $\mathcal H_3$ contains the retained Hardy and core--response terms, and $\mathcal R_N$ is lower order.  The present paper supplies the exact extremal geometry against which such a stability statement should be linearized.

In particular, any future attempt to improve the coefficient further should not repeat the pairwise minimization that produced $0.8941\ldots$.  The correct reference profile is now explicit, and deviations from it can be measured in log radius.  This opens the possibility of coupling a quantitative stability estimate for the radial variational problem to local Lieb--Thirring or Pauli spectral pressure estimates.
